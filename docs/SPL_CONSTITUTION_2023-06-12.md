C.  The Commissioner and Rule Changes

1.  For rule changes, a majority (that is, seven (7) votes) of the league is needed for a proposal to pass and change a rule.

2.  In-season, if a matter is unclear in the existing rules or a rule needs changed due to a problem, the Commissioner has the power to bring the matter up for league vote. In-season rule changes put to vote require a majority (that is, seven (7) votes) to pass.

3.  In-season, sometimes special circumstances may require the Commissioner to make a judgement call to an ambiguous issue without bringing the matter up for a league vote. Such decisions are final.

D.  Trades and Trade Challenges

1.  During the Season, trades must be completed via league software.
    Completed trades are subject to a 24-hour review by the SPL owners.
    SPL owners may veto a trade if 60% of the uninvolved teams vote to
    veto the trade. (That is, if two teams complete a trade, then it
    will pass unless six (6) owners vote to veto the trade.)

2.  During the Off-Season, trades must be announced and accepted via
    email to the entire league. Off-Season trades are considered final
    unless an owner chooses to challenge it within 72 hours. If an owner
    challenges an Off-Season trade, then owners will vote on it. Six (6)
    veto votes are required to veto the trade.

E.  Divisions and Divisional Draft

1.  There will be four (4) divisions. Each division will consist of
    three (3) teams.

2.  Divisions will be selected each Off-Season during the **Divisional
    Draft**

3.  The draft order for the Divisional Draft will be as follows:

    a.  Best record to miss the playoffs

    b.  Second best record to miss the playoffs

    c.  Wildcard loser on the side of the bracket without the SPL Champ

    d.  Wildcard loser on the side of the bracket with the SPL Champ

4.  Divisional Draft Rules:

    a.  Snake draft. The team who goes fourth also goes fifth.

    b.  Rather than selecting an owner to join one's own division, one
        may opt to assign an owner to another owner's division. A given
        owner may only have up to one team assigned to his division in
        this manner.

F.  Slot Draft

1.  The Slot Draft is used to determine the draft order for the SPL
    Draft.

2.  The order of selection for the Slot Draft is as follows:

    a.  Choices 1-6: Inverse order of standings for non-playoff teams.
        That is, the best team to miss the playoffs earns the #1 choice
        in the Slot Draft, the second best team to miss the playoffs
        chooses at #2, and so on.

    b.  Choices 7-12: Final standings order. The SPL Champ chooses at
        #12. The runner-up chooses at #11. The owner who lost to the SPL
        Champ in Week 15 chooses at #10. The owner who lost to the
        runner up chooses at #9. The owner who lost in the Wildcard
        Round on the SPL Champ's side of the bracket chooses at #8. The
        owner who lost in the Wildcard Round on the runner-up's side of
        the bracket chooses at #7.

G.  Playoffs

1.  Seeding for Playoff Spots 1-5 is determined by the following
    sequence in order, the **Tie Breaker Rules**:

    a.  first by winning percentage,

    b.  if needed second by points scored,

    c.  and if needed third by head-to-head record,

    d.  and if needed fourth by coin-flip

2.  The #6 Seed (that is, the second wildcard team) is determined by the
    most points scored (after excluding the first five seeds). Then if
    needed, by head-to-head record, then if needed by coin-flip.

I.  Free Agents

    a.  Blind bid auction

    b.  Each team gets \$200 of fake money for use throughout the season
        and playoffs.

    c.  Refer to league website for schedule of auctions and free agent

    d.  After Week 14, non-playoff teams are excluded from adding Free

    e.  Free Agents added after Week 14 are ineligible for new keeper
        contracts. However, if a free agent is rostered after Week 14

        the player to the new owner. (Note, that contract is still
        considered valid and not terminated.)

J.  Keeper Rules

    a.  On **Keeper Signing Day**, each SPL team gets 10 **Keeper
        Points** ("KP").

    b.  KP may not be traded.

    c.  On Keeper Signing Day, each owner may only carry three keepers
        (the **Three Keeper Max**). The Three Keeper Max only applies on
        Keeper Signing Day.

    d.  All new keeper contracts are executed on Keeper Signing Day.
        When signing Keepers, it is necessary to sign them to a
        contract. Contacts may be for 1, 2, or 3 years. The term of the
        contract is fixed and must be announced at the time of signing.

    e.  The cost to sign each Keeper to a contract is defined in the
        chart, below.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Round Player was drafted in previous SPL Draft** **Year One Cost
                                                     (KP)**
  -------------------------------------------------- --------------------
  First                                              10

  Second                                             8

  Third                                              6

  Fourth or Fifth                                    4

  Sixth, Seven, or Eighth                            3

  Ninth Round or Later                               2

  Undrafted Free Agent                               2
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

f.  The cost for the second year of a contract is equal to (Year 1
    cost) + 1 KP.

g.  The cost for the third year of a contract is equal to (Year 2
    cost) + 1 KP.

h.  No extensions! Once a contract expires, that player cannot be kept
    as a keeper. That player will return to the pool of eligible players
    for the next SPL Draft.

i.  Cut Contracts. If a keeper is dropped from his SPL roster, then the
    keeper contract is immediately null and void. All penalties
    hereinafter defined apply. If that owner or any other picks up the
    player, it does not change the contract status. The contract is
    still null and any penalties are the responsibility of the original
    owner who cut the keeper. This player is ineligible for a keeper
    contract at the end of the season.

j.  If a keeper under contract is not on any owner's roster at the end
    of the Season, then the owner who most recently rostered that player
    is responsible for the contract. Furthermore, since that keeper was
    not on the owner's roster (i.e., an "**Unrostered Keeper"),** that
    keeper cannot be retained. The keeper's contract is considered
    terminated. (See Contact Termination, below.)

k.  **Contract Termination**. A keeper's contract may be terminated in
    two ways: (i) see above for an Unrostered Keeper. (ii) At Keeper
    Signing Day, the owner with the keeper on his roster may elect to
    terminate the contract.

l.  A player whose contract was terminated is not eligible for a
    contract on Keeper Signing Day and will enter the upcoming SPL
    Draft. If that player is then subsequently drafted or acquired via
    free agency, the owner who acquires that player does not assume any
    responsibility for any remaining Cap Penalty liability associated
    with the old terminated contract. The player is considered a normal
    player and is eligible for a new contract in the upcoming
    Off-Season.

m.  **Cap Penalty**. A contract termination results in a Cap Penalty.
    The Cap Penalty is equal to (0.5) \* (KP cost scheduled for that
    year under the contract). The Cap Penalty applies to each remaining
    year on the terminated contract. (For example, imagine a 2 KP keeper
    who is signed to a three-year contract in Year One. The Year One
    price is 2 KP. If the owner decided to terminate the contract at the
    next Keeper Signing Day, then the Cap Penalty in Year Two would be
    1.5 KP and the Cap Penalty in Year Three would be 2 KP.

n.  Cap Penalties are subtracted from an owner's 10 KP total before he
    is able to spend them on current contracts or new contracts.

o.  The Cap Penalty liability with a terminated contract may be traded.
    The contract liability must be traded in whole, not in part. Cap
    Penalties and traded terminated contracts do not count toward the
    Three Keeper Max.

p.  The **Franchise Tag**. The Franchise Tag is a special type of keeper
    contract which may be 1, 2, or 3 years of fixed term. Like a normal
    keeper, Franchise Tag keeper contracts are signed on Keeper Signing
    Day, and Franchise Tag keepers count toward the Three Keeper Max.
    The Franchise Tag has two parts to its cost: (i) In year one, the
    cost includes KP based on normal contract rules and (ii) the
    expenditure of the owner's original first round pick from the
    upcoming SPL Draft. Note, the Franchise Tag only requires KP for the
    first year of the contract. However, the loss of the owner's
    original first round pick applies to every year during the term of
    the Franchise Tag contract. Note, an owner is not permitted to trade
    his first round pick if he has a Franchise Tag keeper.

K.  **SPL Shoot Out**

    a.  Week 14 has a special price (see Dues and Prizes, above) for the
        Week 14 highest score. This prize is in addition to the Free
        Agent Fee weekly high score prize.

    b.  After the Week 13 games, before Week 14 starts, the league shall
        conduct a special draft, the "**SPL Shoot Out Draft**", to
        select Week 14 opponents.

    c.  Week 14 results count toward final regular season standings.

    d.  See Appendix Three for the SPL Shoot Out Draft Order and more
        SPL Shoot Out rules.
